An Open Letter to the Yu-Gi-Oh! Community

To duelists, judges, developers, content creators and the designers at Konami HQ,

For a pretty amount of years, I have been investing my thoughts, passion, and effort into a single idea: bringing a universal algebraic notation to Yu-Gi-Oh!

This is directly inspired by the history of chess.

When looking back, chess was played informally for centuries across the world, but it only began developing into a serious sport after algebraic chess notation was created. Notation changed everything. It allowed players and theorists to record matches for future reference, study playlines across generations without requiring both players in the room, build opening theory, and eventually develop computational engines. Without notation, chess would have remained an ephemeral game lost to the moment it was played. Notation gave it permanence, scientific depth, and immortality.

I have played Yu-Gi-Oh! for more than ten years, and throughout all this time, I have never seen an open, widely adopted notation for our game. Today, our competitive match history is either trapped in unstructured browser clickstreams filled with interface noise, locked inside closed binary replay files that permanently desynchronize whenever a simulator updates its card database or even in simply filmed matches on the Internet. Yu-Gi-Oh! is one of the most complex, skill-intensive, and mechanically deep games ever created. Its intricate chain links, priority windows, non-linear resource lines, and micro-decisions deserve the exact same analytical rigor that chess players have enjoyed for over a century. A universal algebraic notation has the power to catapult our game in that exact same direction, unlocking chess-style game reviews, blunder detection, instant judge dispute resolution, and big-data tournament analytics.

This is not the first idea I have had for improving data analytics in Yu-Gi-Oh! though, but it is certainly a major step forward. My genuine intention is to put Yu-Gi-Oh! toward a more professional, analytical direction and open entirely new possibilities for game evaluation, theory, and long-term study. I am sharing this specification openly because a standard belongs to the community. I warmly invite duelists, judges, platform creators, and tournament organizers to critique, refine, and build upon this foundation together.

And... Who knows? Maybe one day I may be elegible to contribute meaningfully to this game at the highest level, and take a place in game development and data architecture inside Konami headquarters, helping shape the competitive future of the game we all love.

Best Regards,
Creator of the YGN Standard
October 6, 2026
